Sources MS2

Sources MS2

Every number in Milestone 2, where it came from, and how we used it. Company statements are labeled “self-reported,” and projections are labeled as projections. Milestone 1 sources are on Sources MS1.

Scope

What we evaluatedFive solutions already running in the Philippines: platform packaging programs and rules, the Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Act or Republic Act No. 11898, Quezon City’s Trash to Cashback, Republic Act No. 9003 (RA 9003) and barangay materials recovery facilities (MRFs), and city plastic ordinances. Six proposals are scored separately on What’s Missing?
RelevanceDoes each measure address the single-use plastic added for shipping: poly mailers, bubble wrap, air pillows, tape and labels? Most were written for plastic or waste in general.
WhereThe Philippines. Quezon City is our local example because it has the best-documented programs. Taiwan, South Korea, the European Union, India, Singapore and Germany appear only as benchmarks; they are checked on the same three questions but not scored.
How we judgedEffective (a measured change against a baseline) and sustainable (legal basis, funding, duration, who pays). Each measure is scored 1–5 on Ekvall’s five evaluation criteria plus “relevance to e-commerce packaging,” counted twice (Ekvall, 2019). Every score is tied to a published source, and the ranking is checked with equal weights.
What counts as evidenceLaws, government audits and statements, official city pages, peer-reviewed studies and reputable news. Company statements are used but labeled “self-reported.”
WhenMost sources are from 2020 onward. The Quezon City bag ordinance data (2012–2018) is older and is flagged where it appears.

Limitations: what we couldn’t measure

  • No results specific to e-commerce packaging. None of the five solutions publishes how much e-commerce plastic packaging it recovered or prevented. Every result we show covers a wider group of materials.
  • EPR numbers come from companies. They are third-party audited, but each company declares its own footprint, the auditor is paid by the company, and the split between recycling and burning is not published (Vyas et al., 2026).
  • Two EPR figures for 2024. Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) said in January 2026 that rigid plastic recovery was 55.60%; the audited figure announced in August 2026 is 59.98%. We use the later, audited one (Wang, 2026; Cabalza, 2026).
  • MRF coverage mixes methods. The 2021 figure is a government audit; the 2025 figure is a DENR statement. The article gives 49.3%; our math with 42,046 barangays gives 46.3% (Commission on Audit, 2023; Context.ph, 2025).
  • Trash to Cashback mixes materials. The 869,722.93 kg total covers every accepted material, and collected is not the same as recycled (Quezon City Government, 2026).
  • Platform results are not public. The only platform numbers we found are projections and company claims. Shopee’s packing guide is a rule, not a result (Shopee Philippines, 2026).
  • Other countries measure different things. Their recycling rates cover all waste or all packaging, so we compare how their systems work, not their percentages.
  • Scores are judgments. Each 1–5 score applies a published rubric to published evidence, and the reason is shown beside it. Another reader could weigh the same evidence differently. Proposal scores are expected values, not results.
  • No interviews. Stakeholder views come from news reports, official statements and published studies. Where no statement was found, the site says so.

How we adjusted some claims

Claims we used or checked, but not in the exact form we found them.

Claim we foundWhat we use instead
“Recovery” read as recyclingRecovery under EPR also counts co-processing (burning in cement kilns). We say so wherever the EPR rate appears.
“A small seller’s mailer has no owner under EPR”Too absolute. We say small businesses are exempt, so no one is clearly required to recover the mailers they add.
“An MRF in every barangay”RA 9003 allows one MRF per barangay or cluster of barangays, so we measure “barangays served.”
155 firms “violating” EPRA show-cause letter is not a finding of violation. We say “firms DENR suspects are not complying.”
3 kg ≈ 97 ordersLabeled as an illustration: the 31 g per order is a model assumption, not a measured average.
Grab: “almost 60% opted out of cutlery”That was a 2019 pilot in Malaysia only. We use Grab’s 2023 regional figure instead.
Zomato: “cutlery requests fell 74%”The report says 74% of orders went without cutlery after the default changed. We use that wording.
European Commission & GIZ profile dated 2024The document is © 2025, so we cite 2025.
Braganza (2019)The journal’s auto-citation says 2019, but the paper is dated 2017 (Vol. LXI), so we cite 2017.
Quezon City FAQ cited as “n.d.”The FAQ shows a publication date of April 12, 2022.
EU packaging rules “not yet in force”Corrected: Regulation (EU) 2025/40 applies from 12 August 2026; only the 50% empty-space rule waits until 2030 or later.
Taiwan: packaging fell 12% “because of” the rulesThe source reports a 12% year-on-year drop six months after the rules began, with limited inspections. We report the drop, not the cause.

What we left out, and why

Claim seen elsewhereWhy it's not on this site
Taiwan: “a 60% recycling rate”A TaiwanPlus news video (July 2025) states it, but gives no year and no data source, and the official statistics page could not be opened. The site compares methods, not recycling rates, so it is not needed.
“20,458 MRFs in 2025”Conflicts with DENR’s own figure of 12,864, and we could not confirm it at the source.
Quezon City’s “2,778,734.23 million pesos worth”The wording on the program page looks malformed (it would mean about ₱2.8 trillion). We leave it out until the city clarifies.
Japan: “0.8% direct landfill”The source says “landfill waste without intermediate treatment.” Japan is not on the site.
Vietnam as a benchmarkIts packaging EPR (Decree 08/2022) is a general law much like RA 11898 and has no e-commerce provision we could find, so it adds no new method.
Lazada’s “70,000 alternative pouches” (2020)Found only in one outlet whose byline and page details disagree.
The workbook’s AHP rankingIts weights were one member’s starting values, not a group decision. The report card uses simpler, visible scores.
